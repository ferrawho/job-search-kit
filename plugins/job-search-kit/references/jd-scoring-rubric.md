# JD Scoring Rubric

Score out of 100. Every point awarded must cite evidence from a profile file.

| Category | Weight | What earns points |
|---|---|---|
| Must-have requirements | 35 | Each stated requirement met with evidence. Missing hard requirements cost the most. |
| Core responsibilities | 25 | Overlap between the day-to-day in the JD and what the candidate actually does now or has done. |
| Seniority and scope | 15 | Level, team size, budget, and ownership match. Penalize both stretch-too-far and step-down. |
| Domain / industry | 10 | Same or adjacent industry, customer type, business model. |
| Tools and stack | 10 | Named tools the candidate has used. Adjacent tools earn half. |
| Logistics | 5 | Location, remote policy, comp range vs. salary floor, travel. |

## Recommendation thresholds
- **75+: Apply.** Strong fit.
- **60-74: Apply if** the gaps can be handled in the cover letter or are nice-to-haves.
- **Below 60: Skip** unless there is a strategic reason (dream company, referral). Say why.

## Red flags to call out
- Three jobs in one posting, or a title that doesn't match the responsibilities
- Years of experience that don't match the level or salary
- No salary range where pay transparency laws require one
- Signals of churn: "fast-paced", "wear many hats" with no support, reposted often
- Vague success measures or no clear owner of the role
- Required tools or credentials the candidate can't get quickly

## Output format (score.md)
1. Score and one-line verdict
2. Table: category, points, evidence (profile file and the specific fact)
3. Must-have gaps (honest list)
4. Red flags
5. Top 3 talking points to lead with
6. Recommendation: Apply / Apply with caveats / Skip
