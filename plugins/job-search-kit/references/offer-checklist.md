# Offer Review Checklist

Not legal advice. For termination, non-compete, or equity questions with real money at stake, a one-hour review by a local employment lawyer is usually worth it.

## 1. Everything promised is in writing
Compare the offer letter, recruiter emails, and the contract line by line. Look for an **entire agreement** clause: it can cancel anything not written in the contract itself.
- Base salary, pay frequency (semi-monthly vs. bi-weekly)
- Bonus: target, how it's measured, whether it's guaranteed in year one
- Equity: number of shares/options, strike price, vesting schedule, cliff, exercise window after leaving, what happens on acquisition. Often needs a separate grant agreement.
- Benefits: health provider and coverage, retirement match and plan type
- Vacation days and how they grow
- Learning budget, equipment, remote stipend, tools
- Start date, title, reporting line, location/remote terms

## 2. Clauses to read closely
- **Termination without cause:** notice or severance owed. Some contracts limit it to the legal minimum.
- **Active employment clauses:** bonus or vesting stops on the termination date, not the end of the notice period.
- **Non-compete / non-solicit:** length, geography, how narrowly the industry is defined.
- **IP assignment:** list prior inventions and side projects you want to keep.
- **Probation period**
- **Conditions:** background check, references, work authorization. Don't resign your current job until conditions clear.
- **Clawbacks:** sign-on bonus or relocation repayment.

## 3. Total compensation
Base + expected bonus + equity value (state the assumptions) + retirement match + benefits value, compared with the current job and the salary floor.

## 4. Negotiation
- Ask for everything in one round. Lead with excitement, then the asks.
- Anchor with data only when it has a source: market ranges with a cited source, and a competing offer only if you really have one. Never imply an offer you don't have.
- Priorities: what matters most, what's a nice-to-have, what's a walk-away.
- Get final terms in writing before signing.
